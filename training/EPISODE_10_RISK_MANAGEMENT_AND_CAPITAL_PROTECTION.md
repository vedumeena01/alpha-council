## 1. Introduction & The Core Philosophy of Survival
- **"Knowledge is only potential power; it becomes true power only when applied constructively."**
- A trader can win $80\%$ of trades (8 out of 10), but if the remaining 2 losing trades are undisciplined (uncontrolled position size or widened stop-loss), the account will bleed into net loss.
- **Capital Protection Precedes Capital Growth:** Before learning how to grow capital, a trader must master how to protect capital.
- **The Free Fall Phenomenon (Adani Case Study):** When stocks fall violently without technical demand zones on monthly, weekly, or quarterly charts, there are no institutional buying brakes to arrest the fall.

## 2. The Fatal Flaw of Conventional Retail Sizing
Novice traders routinely buy arbitrary share quantities based purely on nominal share price:
- 100 shares of a ₹500 stock.
- 50 shares of a ₹1,000 stock.
- 500 shares of a ₹100 stock.

### The Mathematical Distortion:
- **Trade A (₹1,000 Stock, ₹20 SL, 100 Shares):** Hits 1:1 target (+₹20 gain) $\implies +\mathbf{₹2,000}$.
- **Trade B (₹1,000 Stock, ₹40 SL, 100 Shares):** Hits stop-loss (-₹40 loss) $\implies -\mathbf{₹4,000}$.
- **Outcome:** Win Rate $= 50\%$ (1 Win, 1 Loss), yet the trader sits at a net loss of $-\mathbf{₹2,000}$!
- **The Core Rule:** Whether a stock costs ₹100, ₹1,000, or ₹10,000, and whether the stop-loss is ₹5, ₹20, or ₹65, the monetary loss on any stop-loss hit must always remain **strictly identical and pre-defined**.

---

## 3. The Mathematical Engine: Position Sizing Formula

$$\mathbf{\text{Quantity (Number of Shares)}} = \left\lfloor \frac{\mathbf{\text{Risk Per Trade (RPT)}}}{\mathbf{\text{Absolute Risk Distance (Points)}}} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{\text{Total Capital} \times \text{Risk Tier \%}}{|\text{Entry Price} - \text{Stop-Loss Price}|} \right\rfloor$$

### Risk Per Trade (RPT) Hierarchy:
1. **Learning / Practice Phase:** $0.5\%$ of capital (₹500 on ₹1 Lakh / ₹50 on ₹10,000). Absorbs mistakes cheaply during initial practice.
2. **Beginner Phase (First 1–3 Months):** $1.0\%$ of capital (₹1,000 on ₹1 Lakh).
3. **Intermediate Phase:** $1.5\%$ of capital (₹1,500 on ₹1 Lakh).
4. **Pro Phase (After 3–6 Months Consistency):** $2.0\%$ of capital (₹2,000 on ₹1 Lakh).
5. **Absolute Ceiling:** Never exceed $2.0\%$ risk per trade.

## 4. Critical Execution Errors That Destroy Capital

1. **Chasing Missed Entries (FOMO Buying):**
   - When price touches the proximal line and rallies without filling a limit order, chasing entry expands stop-loss distance and shrinks target distance, causing the Risk-to-Reward Ratio to collapse below 1:1.
   - *Rule:* If an entry is missed, let it go. Missing a trade costs ₹0.
2. **Modifying / Widening Stop-Losses Post-Entry:**
   - Once an order fills, stop-loss price and quantity must **never** be widened or tampered with.
   - Widening an SL from ₹20 to ₹157 on 50 shares turns a ₹1,000 risk into a catastrophic ₹7,850 loss.
3. **The "Averaging Down" Trap:**
   - Buying additional shares as price plunges through a demand zone compounds risk exponentially, transforming a controlled loss into an account wipeout.

## 5. The 100-Trade Directive & The 10 Trades/Month Rule

- **The 100-Trade Commitment:** A valid statistical edge cannot be evaluated on 5 or 10 trades. A trader must execute at least 100 disciplined trades across bull, bear, and sideways cycles.
- **The 10 Trades/Month Cap:** Traders are strictly capped at taking a maximum of **10 trades per calendar month**.
  - *Why?* Restricting trade volume forces ruthless rejection of mediocre setups; only pristine institutional setups survive.
- **Unified Capital Model:**
  - Avoid arbitrary capital partitioning (e.g. 20% intraday, 30% swing, 50% positional).
  - A unified capital pool recycles funds naturally as trades resolve quickly to targets or stop-losses.

## 6. Quantitative Growth Model (The Excel Matrix)

*Benchmark: ₹1,00,000 Capital, 10 Trades/Month, Minimum 2:1 RRR*

### Model 1: Beginner Phase (1% Risk = ₹1,000 Risk / ₹2,000 Target)
- **50% Win Rate (5W, 5L):** $5 \times ₹2,000 - 5 \times ₹1,000 = +\mathbf{₹5,000\text{ (+5\%/month)}}$ ($\mathbf{+60\%\text{ annual}}$ non-compounded).
- **60% Win Rate (6W, 4L):** $6 \times ₹2,000 - 4 \times ₹1,000 = +\mathbf{₹8,000\text{ (+8\%/month)}}$ ($\mathbf{+96\%\text{ annual}}$ non-compounded).
- **70% Win Rate (7W, 3L):** $7 \times ₹2,000 - 3 \times ₹1,000 = +\mathbf{₹11,000\text{ (+11\%/month)}}$.

### Model 2: Pro Phase (2% Risk = ₹2,000 Risk / ₹4,000 Target)
- **50% Win Rate:** $5 \times ₹4,000 - 5 \times ₹2,000 = +\mathbf{₹10,000\text{ (+10\%/month)}}$.
- **60% Win Rate:** $6 \times ₹4,000 - 4 \times ₹2,000 = +\mathbf{₹16,000\text{ (+16\%/month)}}$.
- **70% Win Rate:** $7 \times ₹4,000 - 3 \times ₹2,000 = +\mathbf{₹22,000\text{ (+22\%/month)}}$.

### Model 3: Expanding to 3:1 RRR (2% Risk = ₹2,000 Risk / ₹6,000 Target)
- **50% Win Rate:** $5 \times ₹6,000 - 5 \times ₹2,000 = +\mathbf{₹20,000\text{ (+20\%/month)}}$.
- **60% Win Rate:** $6 \times ₹6,000 - 4 \times ₹2,000 = +\mathbf{₹28,000\text{ (+28\%/month)}}$.
- **70% Win Rate:** $7 \times ₹6,000 - 3 \times ₹2,000 = +\mathbf{₹36,000\text{ (+36\%/month)}}$.

## 7. Advanced Price Action: Smaller Timeframe Overpowering Higher Timeframe
- **Principle:** When a smaller timeframe demand zone (e.g. Daily or 125m) breaches an opposing higher timeframe supply zone (Weekly or Monthly) and closes decisively above it, that smaller timeframe demand zone becomes extraordinarily powerful.
- **Institutional Logic:** A smaller institutional player that overpowers and clears resting sell orders from a macro player has demonstrated exceptional underlying buying strength (`is_htf_overpowering_origin = True`).
